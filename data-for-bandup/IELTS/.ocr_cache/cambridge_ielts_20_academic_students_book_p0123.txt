From the 1950s into the 1970s, O'Keeffe travelled widely, making trips to Asia, the 
Middle East, and Europe. Flying in planes inspired her last two major series – aerial 
views of rivers and expansive paintings of the sky viewed from just above clouds. 
In both series, O'Keeffe increased the size of her canvases, sometimes to mural 
proportions, reflecting perhaps her newly expanded view of the world. When in 
1965 she successfully translated one of her cloud motifs to a monumental canvas 
measuring 6 metres in length (with the help of assistants), it was an enormous 
challenge and a special feat for an artist nearing eighty years of age. 
The last two decades of the artist's life were relatively unproductive as ill health 
and blindness hindered her ability to work. O'Keeffe died in 1986 at the age of 
ninety-eight, but her rich legacy of some 900 paintings has continued to attract 
subsequent generations of artists and art lovers who derive inspiration from these 
very American images.
