It really depends. Generally speaking, friends tend to relate better to 
personal choices like career shifts or dating dilemmas. For example, if you're torn 
between settling down and focusing on your career, friends might give unbiased 
advice, whereas parents would probably push for marriage. That said, when it 
comes to major moves like relocating abroad, cutting off contact with your family 
isn't a wise option, as you need their support in many ways, such as practical input 
on visa arrangements, financial support, or even childcare help. At the end of the 
day, it's a balancing act.

Well, definitely. To be honest, it's completely normal to get ideas from 
friends when planning ahead. I mean, seeing people you trust do something similar 
does give that initial sense of security. But the thing is, if we just mirror their

choices for instance: I've noticed that loads of students go into fields like 
marketing or computer science simply because their social circle does. The 
problem kicks in when they realise their strengths don't align with the coursework 
at all; you can imagine the stress that follows. That's why I firmly believe that when 
it comes to major life decisions, we should build plans around our natural talents 
and core values. After all, we're all wired differently.
